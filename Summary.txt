Summary:
We verified the full workflow for our PyTorch CNN, which classifies photos as military aircraft, commercial airliners,
 or `neither`, the category for birds and drones. The notebook selects a reproducible dataset subset, preprocesses the
 images, and either trains the CNN or loads a saved checkpoint. It displays a confusion matrix, reports precision and
 recall by class, and breaks out bird and drone results separately. After testing class weighting and two fine-tuning
 approaches, we bundled the 1× fine-tuned checkpoint and made it the notebook default. We verified that it correctly
 classified **399 of 445 images (89.7%)** on the existing test split, compared with **388 of 445 (87.2%)** for the
 original model. It also correctly labeled **59 of 77** bird and drone photos, up from **45 of 77**. We kept the
 original checkpoint selectable because it had the highest validation accuracy. We updated the README with setup and
 execution instructions, expected outputs, correctness checks, and team progress. Our main remaining task is to compare
 the models on a fresh labeled photo set to see whether the improvement holds.

Challenges Encountered:
-The training set had 616 neither images, compared with 1,472 in each aircraft class. Fine-tuning improved bird and
drone recognition from 45 to 59 correct out of 77, with 18 still labeled as military.
-The original model led on validation, while the 1× fine-tune led on the existing test split. That made the choice
of evaluation metric consequential.
-The dataset download and CPU training were time-consuming. Bundled checkpoints let the team demonstrate and verify
inference without repeating training.
